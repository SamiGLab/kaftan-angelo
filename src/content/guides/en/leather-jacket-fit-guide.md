> **Quick answer:** Check fastening, shoulders, sleeves and everyday movement with your intended layer underneath. The size label alone is not enough.

A leather jacket fits well when it is comfortable not only while standing still, but also while moving.

## Shoulders
For a conventional set-in sleeve, check the seam near your shoulder line. Dropped shoulders and raglan cuts are designed differently; do not judge every jacket by one seam position.

## Chest and body
You should be able to close the jacket without excessive pulling, while avoiding too much loose volume.

## Sleeve length
Depending on the style, sleeves usually finish around the wrist. Biker styles can be slightly longer.

## Layering
If you will wear it over a sweater, try it that way. The right size depends on how you will actually use the jacket.

## A movement checklist
Fasten the garment, sit down and reach as if holding a steering wheel. Check collar pressure, armhole comfort and strain on the zip. Take ordinary steps in a longer coat. Compare a neighbouring size or another cut where available.

## Adapt the fitting to winter layers
Bring your intended sweater when trying shearling or fur. Assess the weight, lining and body length together. Do not choose a painfully tight garment expecting stretching to fix it.

## Common questions
**Is my usual size enough?** A label is a starting point; compare the actual cut.

**Should I size down because it is leather?** Do not rely on stretching to solve poor fit.
