> **Quick answer:** Read the care label, identify the finish, avoid direct heat and use a specialist for uncertain stains or treatments.

Genuine leather is a natural material, so it does not behave like a synthetic jacket. The main rule is simple: **do not soak it, do not dry it on a radiator, and do not use unknown aggressive cleaners.**

## 1. Dust and surface cleaning
Start with the care label and identify the surface finish. Use a soft dry cloth where the instructions permit. Do not assume that a damp cloth or a product marked “for leather” is suitable for suede, nubuck and coated surfaces alike.

## 2. If the jacket gets wet
Let it dry naturally on a hanger at room temperature. Direct heat can dry the leather out and make it stiff.

## 3. Storage
Use a wide hanger and avoid compressing it for long periods. Leather benefits from ventilation.

## 4. Conditioning
Use a conditioner suitable for the specific leather type and test it on a discreet area first.

## Before asking for cleaning advice
Photograph the care label and the affected area. Note what caused the mark and any product already applied. Ask whether the cleaner handles the specific finish and contrasting trims. Schott NYC recommends specialist leather cleaning and reconditioning; a universal home recipe is not a substitute for garment-specific advice.

## Common questions
**Does every jacket need frequent conditioning?** There is no single interval for all finishes and uses. Follow the garment’s instructions.

**Can I wash it like ordinary clothing?** Do not use a washing machine without explicit instructions for the garment.
