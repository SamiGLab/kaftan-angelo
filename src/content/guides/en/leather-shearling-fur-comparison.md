> **Quick answer:** Compare the actual garment with your intended layers. Weight, lining, coverage, cut and care needs matter alongside the outer material; a category name alone cannot establish warmth.

## Leather jackets
A leather jacket can have a close, structured silhouette or a relaxed everyday cut. Check the lining and room for layers. Leather alone does not specify rain protection or winter warmth. Ask about the finish and care routine before choosing.

## Shearling and irha
Shearling combines a hide surface with a wool side. Inspect the collar, cuffs and lining, then sit and reach in the garment. Different lengths, thicknesses and cuts can feel quite different within the same category.

## Fur coats and vests
Consider the hair surface, garment weight, lining and how the piece sits over your clothes. A vest and a long coat serve different purposes. Ask about the exact material and care requirements rather than treating all fur styles as interchangeable.

## Common questions
**Which material is always warmest?** The category name is not enough. Construction, coverage and clothing layers affect comfort.

**Is every leather coat waterproof?** Do not assume weather protection without information about the particular garment.
