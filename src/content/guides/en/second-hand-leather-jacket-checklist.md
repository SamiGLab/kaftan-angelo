> **Before buying:** Read the material label, inspect the surface, seams, lining and fastenings, and try the garment with your usual layers. Ask for a specialist assessment before relying on a repair estimate. Do not use fire, scratching, water or chemicals to test someone else's garment.

This is a checklist for assessing a particular piece, not a claim that all new or second-hand garments have the same condition. It does not describe a second-hand buying service at Kaftan Angelo.

## Inspection checklist

| Area | What to check | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Material label | Read the composition and care instructions | Can the seller confirm the material and any repairs? |
| Surface | Look for cracking, peeling, discolouration and worn areas in ordinary light | Is the finish original or has it been treated? |
| Seams | Inspect joins and stressed areas without pulling them apart | Are loose threads or tears assessed for repair? |
| Zips and closures | Open and close gently; do not force a stuck fastening | What repair, if any, has been proposed? |
| Lining and pockets | Look for tears and damaged pocket bags | Can a specialist assess any hidden damage? |
| Fit | Fasten over your intended layers, sit and reach forward | Is any adjustment feasible for this garment? |

## Material identification has limits

Touch, smell and a photograph do not conclusively establish a material. Read the label and request clarification if the description is unclear. Do not cut a seam or damage the surface to inspect it. See [how to tell real leather](/en/guides/how-to-tell-real-leather/) for further checks.

## Condition and repair decisions

Ask about storage and cleaning history. If there is a persistent odour, staining or surface damage, ask an appropriate specialist what can be assessed or treated before buying. Do not assume cleaning will remove a problem or that repair will cost a fixed amount. Obtain an individual quotation; no repair prices are published here.

A lower purchase price does not establish the final cost of ownership, and a new garment is not automatically a guarantee of fit or future condition. Inspect the actual piece in either case.

## Fit comes before a stretching assumption

Do not buy a tight jacket on the assumption it will stretch to fit. Check shoulder movement, lining tension, sleeve length and the fastened hem with your intended layers. Read our [fit guide](/en/guides/leather-jacket-fit-guide/) for preparation.
